# ordi v1.16.0

CRM kept records. It did not tell anyone what to do today.

Companies, contacts, deals, notes and files were all there, and everything
that had not happened yet lived in a paragraph of a note: "ping them next
week", "waiting on the CTO". Research arrived as a wall of text and got
retyped into the pipeline, so a prospect nobody had spoken to sat in the
same board as a signed proposal and inflated it. Opening CRM answered
"what do we have" and never "who needs attention, what happened last, what
happens next".

This release separates the pursuit from the opportunity and makes the next
action the unit of work.

## Work is what CRM opens on

`/crm` lands on Work: five queues built from planned activities and lead
state - **overdue**, **due today**, **waiting for reply**, **nurture due**
and **no next action**. The last one is the queue that matters most, because
a record with nothing planned is the one that quietly dies.

The queues are owner-aware and answer with exact totals rather than "20+",
including records nobody owns yet, so an unassigned lead is not invisible
work. Leads and deals are classified separately in bounded SQL, and only the
next activity is loaded for the lead and pipeline views instead of every
activity ever logged.

Due today means due today *where the seller is*. The user's timezone travels
in the actor context and day boundaries are computed as local days, DST
included, so a morning in Kyiv and a morning in Lisbon do not disagree about
what is overdue.

## Leads are a workspace, not a pipeline stage

A lead is an unqualified pursuit: a company, an owner, an optional proposed
contact, and the research that justified opening it. Its lifecycle is `new →
needs review → ready → waiting for reply → engaged → nurture → converted`,
with `disqualified` and `no response` as endings. The pipeline holds
qualified deals only.

Lead detail leads with the next action, then outreach, qualification state,
the structured research - signal, evidence, fit, timing, score, source links
and their check date, suggested channel, opener, caution - and a
chronological sales history. Nurture carries its own validated return date,
so "not now" is a date rather than a hope.

Converting a qualified lead creates the deal and keeps everything: company,
contact, research, notes, files, activity history, owner and next action.
Legacy deals sitting in a "Lead" stage move the other way with
`demote_deal_to_lead`, also without losing anything. Navigation back through
a conversion keeps working from both sides.

## Sales activities are first-class

Planned, completed and cancelled, each with a type (review, outreach, follow
up, call, meeting, proposal, nurture, other), owner, due date, outcome and
context. Completing one records the outcome, moves the lead's status and
schedules the follow-up in the same step, without leaving the record.

Every transition is one transaction with its audit record: a lead update
locks the row and commits the status change, the cancellation of planned
activities and the audit entry together, so a failure halfway cannot leave a
lead in one state and its work queue in another. Scheduling against a
nurtured lead brings it back to ready in the same transaction. A planned
activity cannot be created without a date, and one activity belongs to
exactly one lead or one deal - enforced by a check constraint, not by
convention.

## Research imports instead of being retyped

CRM → Leads takes a structured research batch as JSON, previews it before
anything is written, and imports it: companies matched by domain first,
leads deduplicated, field mapping shown, and excluded prospects retained
with their exclusion reason rather than becoming active company records for
someone to clean up later.

## Playbooks: templates and manual sequences

Settings live in CRM → Playbooks. Message templates carry reusable copy with
`{{companyName}}`, `{{contactFirstName}}`, `{{contactName}}`, `{{ownerName}}`
and `{{leadTitle}}`. Sequences are ordered manual steps; enrolling a lead or
deal plans the first action and each completion plans the next.

Sequences never send anything. They schedule human work - ordi stays an
operational CRM rather than a marketing automation suite, and outreach is
sent by the person whose name is on it.

## A morning digest in the seller's own morning

Once per local working morning, each user holding `crm.read` gets their work
summary; deal work is included only for those holding `deals.read`. A run
ledger keyed by user and local date records empty mornings too, so work
created at 2pm does not produce a surprise second "morning" digest. It is a
per-type toggle in Profile → Notifications like every other email.

## Email delivery survives a restart

Notification email used to be sent inside the event consumer: an SMTP
failure lost the mail, and a redelivered event sent it twice. Deliveries are
now rows. The consumer enqueues in the same transaction as the change that
caused it and returns without touching the network; a worker claims due rows,
sends, and retries on a backoff of 1m, 5m, 30m, 2h and 12h before marking the
row dead with its last error. An idempotency key makes an enqueue that runs
twice one delivery, a claim abandoned by a crashed process is reclaimed after
five minutes, and notifications carry a dedupe key so the in-app copy is
written once as well.

## An agent can plan a content calendar in ordi

Reported from an agent run: `create_task` took a title and a priority,
`list_my_tasks` was the only way to see a task, and nothing carried a body, a
date, a status, a label, a source link or a comment. So the calendar lived
outside ordi and got copied in by hand, which is how one post ends up filed
three times with three dates.

The planning surface is the existing model, exposed:

- `get_project_schema` answers a project key ("CONTENT") with the statuses,
  task types, labels and custom fields the write tools accept, so ids stop
  being guesswork. `list_projects` filters by key.
- `list_tasks` reads the calendar - due-date window, status, labels, type,
  title text, custom fields - in date order. The API gained the filters that
  were missing for it: `dueFrom`/`dueTo`, and `label` as a comma list that
  narrows rather than widens.
- `get_task` opens the whole card: body as text, date, status, labels, custom
  fields, assignees, links, comments, and the version to write back with.
- `create_task` writes all of it in one call, `update_task` rewrites it, and
  `add_task_link` attaches a source before publishing or the permalink after
  (the same url twice is a no-op).

Two properties a repeated run needs:

- **One task per key.** `upsert_task` takes the caller's own key, keeps it in
  a custom field and looks it up before writing, so re-running a generation
  updates that post instead of filing its twin. `create_task` refuses a taken
  `externalKey` and names the task holding it.
- **Hand edits survive.** Every write carries the version it read, so a
  concurrent edit is a 409 that says to re-read rather than a silent
  overwrite. `upsert_task` fingerprints title, text, date and status as it
  wrote them, so a post someone rewrote or moved in ordi is refused until the
  caller passes `force`.

Tool failures carry the API's code plus the move that fixes it: a version
conflict names the re-read and the current version, a duplicate names the
existing ref, a missing project lists the keys the token reaches. Sales work
is reachable the same way - `list_leads`, `get_lead`, `create_lead`,
`update_lead`, `get_sales_work`, `list_sales_activities`,
`schedule_sales_activity`, `update_sales_activity`,
`complete_sales_activity`, `cancel_sales_activity`, `convert_lead`,
`demote_deal_to_lead`, `preview_research_import`, `import_research`,
`list_sales_playbooks`, `save_sales_message_template`, `save_sales_sequence`
and `manage_sales_sequence` - through the same permissions the UI uses.
Deletion stays out, per the catalog's rule.

## Also in this release

- A deleted deal no longer blocks deleting the client. The guard counted
  soft-deleted rows while every other count filtered them out, so deleting a
  deal - or demoting one back to a lead, which soft-deletes it - left a ghost
  dependency and no list on the page explained it. It counts live rows and
  names what blocks with counts ("1 lead and 1 deal") instead of sending the
  user through four empty lists.
- Editing a note saves once. The mousedown listener, the blur handler and
  Cmd+Enter each committed, and clicking a focusable element fires two of
  them before the first PATCH resolves - two writes, two "Saved." toasts.
  All three go through one guarded commit.
- Long knowledge base titles wrap instead of being clipped. The title was a
  single-line input with `truncate`, so anything wider than the column could
  only be read by scrolling inside the field; it is the auto-growing textarea
  task titles already use, and re-fits when the versions panel opens.
- Mixed-currency totals render as their currencies rather than being summed
  into a meaningless number.
- Text written through MCP comes back as it was written: `docToText`
  separates top-level blocks with a blank line, so a body read, edited and
  saved no longer loses its paragraphs.

## Upgrading

Six additive migrations (`0014`–`0019`): leads, sales activities, sales
digest runs, email deliveries, message templates and sequences, plus a
`domain` index on companies and due indexes on activities. `0016`
backfills undated planned activities from their timestamps before making
`due_at` NOT NULL. Nothing is dropped or rewritten, existing CRM routes stay
compatible, and `/companies` and `/deals` still land on the right tab.
