# ordi v1.17.0

v1.16.0 gave the CRM a shape: leads separate from the pipeline, a Work queue,
sales activities as records rather than sentences in a note. Then someone sat
down in front of it as a new seller would, and the shape turned out to have
been drawn around the tool that filled it in.

A lead had a dozen qualification fields and no way to type into any of them,
because an importer had always done that. The queue hid work that was booked
for tomorrow and offered a Complete button on work nobody had done yet. The
convert button appeared only once a lead was already qualified, so the one
question a new seller has - what has to happen before I can convert this? -
had no answer on the page. And the release notes for the importer that caused
this are one file over, in v1.16.0.

So this release takes it out and finishes the parts it was standing in for.
Alongside that, the rich text editor stopped claiming to be Notion-grade in
its own docstring and became it.

## The lead is fillable

Pain signal, why it fits, why now, evidence, caution, the opening message,
product, score, signal, source, source link, suggested channel and the title
are all editable in place on the lead page, and the owner is pickable. Those
fields only ever had an importer to fill them; after it was removed the page
was a dozen boxes a seller could read and never write.

A lead can now also be logged in one pass. The company picker offers
"+ New company…" and the contact picker "+ New contact…", so the usual case -
a prospect who is not in the workspace yet - no longer means cancelling out to
another tab and starting over. Whoever creates a company owns it, the way
creating a lead already worked.

Convert to deal is visible throughout a lead's life and says what it is
waiting for, rather than silently not being there until the lead reaches
Engaged. A converted lead is frozen: its fields stop being editable once the
deal owns them.

## The queue tells the truth about the day

There is a **Booked ahead** bucket. A lead whose next step was planned for
tomorrow matched no bucket at all and vanished from the page, while
**waiting for reply** - the one state that needs nothing from you - was always
shown, with a button offering to complete work days early.

Each row's action now follows its bucket: finish overdue and due-today work,
plan the unplanned, leave the rest to read. The morning digest fires on the
actionable part only, so a fully-planned week no longer produces a daily
email. The queue's empty state offers a way on to Leads - it is the CRM's
landing tab, so on day one it was the first thing a new seller met, and it led
nowhere.

The dashboard counts sales work beside the task counters. `My open tasks`
counts project work, so a seller with a full outreach queue was told they were
all caught up.

## The research import is gone

It froze one external research tool's JSON shape into the database schema and
the public API - snake_case fields in an otherwise camelCase contract - and
carried a hardcoded list of job-board and company-registry hostnames in the
service layer, which is a decision about one company's sourcing habits living
in everyone's install.

Removed with it: `POST /leads/import`, `POST /leads/import/preview`, the
`import_research` and `preview_research_import` MCP tools, the Import research
dialog, the `research_batches` table, and the four `leads` columns that only
ever held import payloads (`research_batch_id`, `dimensions`,
`secondary_sources`, `raw_research`).

Leads are unchanged. They are created through `POST /leads`, the MCP
`create_lead` tool or the New lead dialog, and keep every qualification field
they had - which, as of this release, a human can actually fill in.

## Write paths that were losing writes

A stale edit to a client, contact or deal answers 409 instead of reporting
success and discarding the change. The version filter alone matched zero rows
without anyone noticing, and PATCH returned 200 with the other writer's data.
Each update commits with its audit entry in one transaction, so a rejected
contact edit can no longer demote the previous primary contact on its way out.

Deleting a lead cancels its planned activities and stops its active sequence,
like every other way a lead stops being worked. The enrollment used to stay
`active` forever on a lead nothing could reach, and kept counting toward the
sequence's active total.

`POST /companies/:id/portal` verifies the company exists before minting a
token and records the rotation in the company's history. It used to update by
id and report a fresh token for a deleted or invented id, with nothing in the
audit trail.

`GET /deals` is bounded and paged (`limit`, `cursor`, default 100, max 200);
it used to return every deal in the workspace on every request. `GET
/companies` honours the `nextCursor` it already returned - nothing consumed it
before, so passing it back replayed the first page, and the MCP
`list_companies` tool hands that cursor to a model. Both lists page on the
ULID primary key: ids sort by creation time and compare as exact text, where a
`createdAt` cursor silently lost the microseconds Postgres keeps and matched
no row.

Opening a CRM URL without the permission for it shows a plain no-access page
instead of a screen whose every request 403s. The sidebar already hid those
sections; a typed URL, a restored tab and a bookmark went straight through.

## A Sales role, and a playbook that is not empty

New preset role: **Sales** - the whole CRM plus read-only sight of projects and
finance. Until now the only preset covering the sales workspace was Manager,
which also grants project write and the right to issue and send invoices.

Playbooks ship with a starter: three message templates and a three-step
outreach sequence, seeded like deal stages and editable like any other record.
The tab used to open empty on a feature about reusable copy.

## The editor is finished

Task bodies, KB pages and project descriptions had bold, italic, strike, code,
three heading levels and a ten-item slash menu. They now have underline, text
colour and highlight, alignment, tables with row and column tools, callouts in
four tones, collapsible toggles, code blocks with syntax highlighting across 17
languages, and images.

A hover gutter inserts a block below, drags to reorder, and opens turn into /
move / duplicate / delete. Blocks are declared once in `richtext/blocks.ts`, and
the slash menu, the bubble toolbar's turn-into list and the block handle's menu
are all generated from that table - which is how the previous version drifted.
`RichBody` was a second renderer with its own switch statement, so every block
had to be taught twice; it had never learned tables, colour or highlighting,
and is a pass-through to `RichText` now.

## Images live in the document

Images could only be embedded by url, because nothing in the attachment flow
could be put inside a document: an `<img>` sends no Authorization header, and a
presigned storage url expires within the hour, so a document holding one rots.

A document now stores a signed, non-expiring path - `/api/v1/files/<id>/<token>`,
where the token is an HMAC of the attachment id under `AUTH_SECRET`. That is
the model invoices, quotes and client portals already use: an unguessable token
in the url, served with no session, redirecting to a freshly presigned url so
storage bandwidth never crosses the API. The tradeoff is explicit - whoever
holds the link can fetch the file - and rotating `AUTH_SECRET` invalidates every
issued link. The path is stored root-relative, so moving the instance to
another domain does not break every image ever embedded.

`/attachments/presign` signs the key it issues and `/attachments/register`
refuses a key without a matching signature. Registering an arbitrary key was a
way to mint a public link to any object in the bucket, an invoice PDF included.

Three ways in, one code path: paste a screenshot, drop a file on the page, or
use the Image dialog, which still takes a link too.

## Rows open where you point them

Cmd/Ctrl-click and middle-click open a list row in a second tab. Project, task,
lead, deal, client, invoice, employee, dashboard and subtask rows were plain
click handlers calling `navigate()`, which swallows the modifier, so the
context menu was the only way to open anything beside what you were reading.

The keyboard scheme is one table with a help sheet on `Shift+?`, instead of
three scattered listeners nothing documented. New: `Cmd+T`, `Cmd+Shift+T` to
reopen the last closed tab, `Cmd+1`…`9`, and `Cmd+[` / `Cmd+]` for history. The
G chord reaches 11 destinations.

External links work in the desktop app. Two independent silent failures: the
capability granted `opener:allow-open-url` with no url scope, which rejects
every call, and nothing routed links to the opener anyway - all three webviews
drop a new-window request instead of handing it to the OS. A delegated handler
covers links the SPA renders later too.

## Also in this release

- Planned work no longer reads "now". `fmtRelative` measured
  `now - timestamp`, so every future instant went negative, matched the
  under-a-minute branch and rendered as "now" - in the Work queue, the Leads
  table, the lead page and the sales history. A follow-up due next week and one
  due this minute looked identical, which is the one distinction that queue
  exists to make.
- Unqualified prospects live in the Leads workspace only: the pipeline's legacy
  "Lead" stage is migrated away without losing sales context, and the demotion
  flow it needed is gone from the UI, the API and MCP. A stage may still be
  named "Lead" - the ban that shipped beside that migration was a one-off guard
  promoted into permanent validation.
- Sales activity dates go through the shared date picker and its separate time
  control, so they honour the user's date preferences. Completing a research
  review readies the lead and schedules the first outreach, instead of
  recording that a reply is already pending.
- Web CRM reads its status and activity-type enums from `@ordi/shared` instead
  of keeping copies, so an enum added on the server reaches the dropdowns.
- The Leads table shows the owner, so a team can see whose pipeline is whose
  without opening every record.
- Naming: the Companies tab says "company" throughout, since the list holds
  prospects as well as clients, and the company status `lead` is labelled
  "Prospect" so the word no longer names both a company status and the separate
  Leads workspace beside it.
- Page entrances replay on every navigation and reset their own scroll. They
  only ever keyed off the first path segment, so opening a project from the
  project list animated nothing.
- ordi has a landing page at
  [romirom11.github.io/ordi](https://romirom11.github.io/ordi), hand-written
  HTML and CSS with the design tokens copied from the app.
- CRM internals split up with no behaviour change: `service.ts` was a
  1100-line pile of companies, contacts, deals, leads, activities and
  conversion, and is now a re-export surface over `companies.ts`, `deals.ts`,
  `leads.ts`, `activities.ts` and `common.ts`. Every `input: any` in it is
  typed from its Zod schema.

## Upgrading

Three migrations, and two of them drop things - take a backup first.

- `0020` removes the legacy "Lead" pipeline stage. Unqualified deals sitting in
  it become reviewable leads with their context intact; deals that had already
  been converted from a lead stay deals and move to Qualified.
- `0021` drops the `research_batches` table and the `leads` columns
  `research_batch_id`, `dimensions`, `secondary_sources` and `raw_research`.
  Anything those columns held is not recoverable after it runs.
- `0022` drops the `leads.legacy_deal_id` marker `0020` used.

This is the first release that removes public surface. `POST /leads/import`
and `POST /leads/import/preview` return 404, and the `import_research`,
`preview_research_import` and `demote_deal_to_lead` MCP tools are no longer
advertised - an agent that calls one gets an unknown-tool error. Every other
CRM route is unchanged.

Nothing new is required in the environment. Image links are signed with the
`AUTH_SECRET` you already have; rotating it invalidates links already embedded
in documents.
