# ordi trademark policy

The name **ordi** and the ordi logo are trademarks of

## Why a project this open has a trademark policy

The AGPL gives you broad rights over the *code*: run it, read it, change it,
redistribute it, host it for other people, commercially or not. None of that is
affected by anything on this page, and nothing here is permission you need to
ask for.

Copyright and trademarks are separate things, though, and an open source licence
grants only the first. A licence lets anyone ship modified ordi. Only the
trademark stops modified ordi from being handed to users still calling itself
ordi, so that a bug someone else introduced, or a hosted service someone else
operates badly, lands on this project's name.

GPL-family licences anticipate exactly this: AGPL-3.0 section 7(e) allows a
project to decline to grant trademark rights without that being a further
restriction on the software. This policy is that decline, written out.

**If a term here would ever restrict a right the AGPL gives you over the code,
the AGPL wins and the term does not apply.**

## What you may do without asking

- Say that your product works with, integrates with, is built on, or is
  compatible with ordi.
- Write about ordi, review it, teach it, give talks about it, publish tutorials
  and comparisons, whether or not you are complimentary.
- Redistribute ordi **unmodified** under the name ordi, including in a package
  manager, a container image or a distribution.
- Run ordi for yourself, your company, or your clients, and say that is what you
  are running.
- Use the logo to refer to the project, unaltered and not as the primary mark of
  your own product.

Nominative use – using a name to refer to the thing it names – does not require
permission, and this section is descriptive of that, not a grant that could be
withdrawn.

## What needs permission first

- **Distributing modified ordi under the name ordi.** Fork freely; rename before
  you ship it to others. "Based on ordi" or "a fork of ordi" is accurate and
  fine. Calling the result "ordi" is not.
- **Offering a hosted or managed service under the ordi name**, or under a name
  containing it – "ordi Cloud", "ordi Hosting", "Hosted ordi" and the like.
  Running ordi as a service is your right under the AGPL. Doing it under this
  name is not, because users cannot tell whose service they bought.
- **Domain names, account handles and app store listings** whose distinctive
  element is ordi.
- **Company, product or project names** containing ordi.
- **Merchandise** carrying the name or logo.
- **Anything implying endorsement**, partnership or official status.

## Forks

Forking is welcome and no permission is needed for the code. Two requests when a
fork becomes something users install:

1. Pick a name that is not confusable with ordi, and is not ordi with a prefix
   or suffix bolted on.
2. Say plainly what it is derived from. Attribution helps both projects.

## Using the logo

Do not stretch it, recolour it, rotate it, add effects to it, or combine it with
other marks so that a new logo results. Leave clear space around it. Do not make
it the most prominent mark on a page that is selling something other than ordi.

## Asking

Open an issue titled **Trademark permission** at
<https://github.com/romirom11/ordi/issues> describing what you want to do.
Reasonable requests are granted, usually quickly. Asking is not a trap – most
answers are yes, and the ones that are not usually turn into "yes, under a
different name".

## If this policy changes

Permission already granted in writing stays granted for what it covered. Changes
are not applied retroactively to make someone's existing use infringing.
