# Contributor License Agreement

ordi is AGPL-3.0 software and stays that way. This agreement does not change
that; it makes sure the project is allowed to keep distributing your work.

## Why this exists

Every contributor owns the copyright in what they write. Without a written
grant, a project only has an implied licence to use a contribution – nobody is
quite sure how wide it is, and it cannot be relied on years later.

That matters for ordi in two concrete ways:

- **Relicensing has to stay possible.** If the AGPL ever has to be replaced –
  a licence incompatibility, a later GPL version, an ordi commercial licence
  offered alongside the open one – that needs the agreement of every copyright
  holder. Collecting it from contributors who have long moved on is, in
  practice, impossible. The grant below is given once, up front.
- **Enforcement needs a clear holder.** Someone taking ordi proprietary can
  only be challenged by a party with rights in the code.

In return, section 9 commits the project to keeping ordi under an OSI-approved
open source licence. The agreement is a licence, not an assignment: **you keep
the copyright in everything you write.**

This is the same shape as the Apache Software Foundation's individual CLA, which
most contributors have signed at some point already.

## How to sign

Open your first pull request. A bot comments on it with a link; you sign in with
GitHub and confirm. It takes about thirty seconds and is remembered for every
pull request after that.

If the bot has not commented, say so in the pull request and it will be sorted
out before merge.

## Contributing for a company

If you are contributing as part of your job, and your employment contract
assigns your work to your employer, the individual agreement above is not enough
on its own – your employer needs to grant the same rights. Open an issue and it
will be arranged.
